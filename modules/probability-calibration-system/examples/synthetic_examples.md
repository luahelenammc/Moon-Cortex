# Synthetic Examples

**MSL profile:** 5.1-compatible Markdown-native examples; all scenarios below are fictional, low-stakes, and non-evidentiary.

These examples illustrate the distinctions and output shape. They do not establish a base rate, validate a method, or predict any real person or project.

## 1. High fit, low evidence

**Question:** Will a neighborhood craft workshop fill its 12 places by the registration deadline?

**Context:** A past workshop had a similar topic and venue, so it is a close analogy. The organizer has records for only three events, uses a different registration window this time, and has no current sign-up count.

- **Fit:** High for topic and venue; incomplete for timing and audience.
- **Evidence:** Low. Three selected prior events cannot establish a stable base rate.
- **Probability:** Low to moderate, qualitative only.
- **Confidence:** Low.
- **Next step:** Check current registrations and compare like-for-like events before changing the estimate.

The strong resemblance supports fit. It does not make the sparse record strong evidence.

## 2. Moderate probability, high confidence in the broad band

**Question:** Will the next comparable community workshop reach its minimum attendance?

**Context:** Across 200 consecutive first-time registrations for the same monthly workshop format during a period with no identified process change, 96 participants attended. The outcome and records are complete, and each person appears once. The next workshop uses the same format and registration rules.

- **Fit:** High for format and rules.
- **Evidence:** A large, complete, directly comparable historical record for this limited target.
- **Probability:** Moderate; the observed attendance rate was 96/200 (48%), used here only as a description of that record.
- **Confidence:** High in the broad moderate band under the stated stability assumption; lower in any exact probability for the next workshop.
- **Update triggers:** A change to the audience, schedule, location, registration process, or promotion should prompt review.

The historical fraction is not a guarantee. Confidence concerns the broad estimate, not whether this particular event will succeed.

## 3. Correlated reposts

**Question:** Does a public announcement now have six independent confirmations?

**Context:** The same organizer notice was copied to six community channels. No new response, commitment, or independent source appeared.

- **Evidence:** One announcement replicated across six channels, not six independent observations.
- **Probability:** Do not update the outcome probability from the repost count alone.
- **Confidence:** Unchanged unless the source or underlying facts are independently verified.
- **Next step:** Trace the original notice and look for a direct action tied to the target.

Distribution is not independent confirmation.

## 4. Qualitative only

**Question:** Will a newly launched neighborhood audio series find a steady audience within two months?

**Context:** The definition of a steady audience is not yet agreed, comparable programs are not documented, and the first episode has not been released.

- **Fit:** Not assessed until the target and comparison group are defined.
- **Evidence:** Too limited for a numerical probability.
- **Probability:** Not yet estimable. If the user defines the target, a cautious qualitative band may be possible after the first observations.
- **Confidence:** Low.
- **Next step:** Define a measurable audience threshold and review direct first-party listening data after launch.

Do not invent a percentage to make the answer look complete.

## 5. Recalibration after direct evidence

**Question:** Will the community group have access to its reserved library room at the scheduled time?

**Before update:** The group has a verbal indication but no confirmed reservation. The qualitative estimate is moderate with low-to-moderate confidence.

**New evidence:** The library sends a written reservation confirmation for the correct room and date.

- **Update:** The room-availability estimate moves upward because the new source directly confirms the reservation.
- **Confidence:** Moderate; a confirmation does not rule out a later closure, access issue, or schedule change.
- **What this does not prove:** The email does not establish that the event will be attended or that every operational dependency is settled.
- **Refresh triggers:** A cancellation, building notice, room change, or conflicting official message.

Preserve the original estimate and record the new source, date, and reason for the change. The new observation changes the relevant estimate; it does not prove a broader causal story.
