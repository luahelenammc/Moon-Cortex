## Adaptive setup route

Use this loop:

```text
inspect → infer → ask only material gaps → preserve what works → compile → test
```

If the user has already supplied enough information, proceed. Do not turn setup into an intake form because forms are very good at reproducing the feeling of work.

### 1. Find the actual need

Ask or infer what financial friction the user wants reduced. Examples include:

- not knowing where money goes;
- losing track of cards, installments or future commitments;
- confusing account balance with available money;
- having irregular income or several pay rhythms;
- managing shared household or third-party expenses;
- needing a safer, more legible monthly close;
- repairing an existing finance system without rebuilding it.

The goal is not to diagnose the person. It is to identify the capability the field lacks.

### 2. Inspect existing material

Classify supplied material as current, historical, provisional, duplicated, private, shared or unknown. Identify whether it is a ledger, source, statement, budget, packet, archive or ordinary note.

Preserve a working structure when it already has sound ownership and freshness. Repair or integrate before creating a parallel system.

### 3. Discover the financial field

Ask only the branches that matter.

#### Jurisdiction and currencies

Discover, when material:

- where most financial activity occurs;
- residence, banking, income and tax jurisdictions when they differ;
- primary and secondary currencies;
- cross-border conversion needs;
- local payment and payroll terms.

Do not infer jurisdiction from language, currency symbol or bank brand. Verify current external rules only when a rule changes the model, and mark unverified rules as pending.

#### Income topology

Discover:

- income sources;
- cadence and regularity;
- gross/net distinction when relevant;
- deductions, benefits, bonuses and reimbursements;
- advances and their economic ownership;
- variable or uncertain future income;
- currency and expected timing.

Support monthly, weekly, every-other-week, twice-monthly, freelance, commission, seasonal and mixed income. Do not assume a monthly salary.

#### Cash topology

Identify the containers that represent liquid money in this user's life:

- operating/current-account equivalents;
- savings and liquid reserves;
- wallets, cash and digital balances;
- joint accounts;
- multiple-currency containers;
- other liquid vehicles only when operationally relevant.

Do not impose a country-specific account vocabulary.

#### Credit and debt topology

Activate only instruments that exist:

- credit or charge cards;
- overdraft or credit lines;
- installment purchases;
- buy-now-pay-later arrangements;
- personal, education, housing, tax, institutional or informal debt;
- other local liabilities.

If the user has no card, do not materialize card intelligence merely because this bootstrap knows how to spell “installment.”

#### Obligation topology

Discover only material families, such as housing, utilities, insurance, subscriptions, taxes, debt service, care, healthcare, education, transport, professional costs and shared household expenses.

Do not moralize functional spending. Classification serves clarity, not punishment.

#### Shared-money topology

Determine whether the user pays for others, expects reimbursement, uses joint money, manages household costs, receives offsets or carries obligations belonging economically to another person.

Activate third-party or shared-cost ledgers only when they change reconciliation or decisions.

#### Reserve and asset topology

Identify operational reserves such as emergency funds, tax reserves, planned purchases, annual obligations, healthcare or care reserves and other liquidity-relevant assets.

Ask which reserves are protected, conditional or flexible. A named protected reserve is not free cash because it happens to be visible in an account.

#### Financial rhythm

Discover paydays, statement closes, due dates, recurring billing clusters, reimbursement lags, settlement rhythms and the user's natural financial cycle.

Possible cycle forms include calendar month, paycheck-to-paycheck, primary-payday cycle, statement/due cycle or a custom cycle. A non-monthly earner must not be forced into a model that mislabels an occasional extra paycheck as sustainable monthly income.

#### Safety and behavioral calibration

Determine proportionately:

- what makes the user feel safer;
- how much visible cash matters;
- discomfort with debt or open statements;
- tolerance for low current balances;
- card and automation comfort;
- detail tolerance;
- tendency to lose small transactions;
- categories that require structural protection.

High frequency does not imply waste. Read function, need, accessibility, recurrence and replaceability before suggesting compression.

### 4. Discover the host

Before materializing the local system, determine where it can live:

- AI/model environment;

## Accounting and state contract

The generated system must preserve, when relevant:

- credit purchase creates future liability; it is not immediate cash outflow;
- statement payment is cash outflow plus liability settlement, not a second economic expense;
- internal transfer changes position, not income or expense;
- expected reimbursement is not cash before receipt;
- protected reserve is not free cash;
- prepayment reduces liability but does not erase gross transaction history;
- an obligation must not be double-counted across cash, transfer, bill, credit, reserve or reimbursement;
- advances and irregular income are classified by economic ownership, not visual account balance alone;
- open statements are observed ground, not automatically final truth;
- unresolved reconciliation gaps remain visible.

When a cycle is reconciled, show the smallest useful equation:

```text
opening state
+ confirmed inflows
- confirmed cash outflows
± internal movements where position changes
± credits, refunds and adjustments
= closing state
```

If it does not balance, show the difference and candidate explanations only when evidenced. Never invent a balancing transaction.

## Privacy gate

Never request or store passwords, PINs, CVVs, seed phrases, private keys, tokens, full card numbers or unnecessary full account numbers.

Prefer aliases, masking, ranges and role labels. Keep third-party private data out unless necessary, authorized and appropriately protected. Do not put confidential financial material in global AI instructions merely because a destination field is convenient.

Public output must not contain real personal salaries, balances, debts, statements, account identifiers, family ledgers or private source links.

## Financial Reality Test

Installation is not complete when files are merely written. Use the proportional test in [`docs/FINANCIAL_REALITY_TEST.md`](docs/FINANCIAL_REALITY_TEST.md).

At minimum, demonstrate where relevant:

- opening cash;
- confirmed income;
- credit purchase;
- liability and statement payment;
- expected reimbursement;
- internal transfer;
- protected reserve;
- closing cash;
- unresolved difference.

The essential assertions are:

```text
credit purchase ≠ immediate cash outflow
statement payment = cash outflow + liability settlement
expected reimbursement ≠ current cash
internal transfer ≠ spending
protected reserve ≠ free cash
```

If a test fails, repair the installation or mark it `PARTIAL`; do not upgrade partial output to success.
