# Synthetic Example B · Credit and Non-Monthly Pay User

**Status:** hypothetical / fictional / didactic / non-evidentiary  
**Module:** Moon Cortex · Financial Living System `0.1.0-pre.4`  
**Bootstrap:** Adaptive Finance Bootstrap  
**MSL:** 4.3

This example demonstrates why a calendar-month budget can misread a biweekly earner with credit tails. It is fictional, not a case study.

## Field supplied

The fictional user says:

- net pay usually arrives every two weeks;
- some calendar months contain a third pay event, but the user does not want to treat it as permanent monthly income;
- two credit cards are used for different functions;
- both cards carry inherited installments from prior decisions;
- many small transport and convenience transactions are hard to see in the statement;
- the user paid a shared household purchase and expects repayment next week;
- the current open statements are visible, but the final amounts are not closed.

The user asks: “How much of the statement is actually new pressure, and what will hit the next pay cycle?”

## Field model

- **Income:** regular every-other-week, with variable calendar-month count;
- **Cash:** operating account, savings and card payment reserve;
- **Credit:** two cards, current purchases and inherited installment tails;
- **Obligations:** recurring bills, statement liabilities and shared household purchase;
- **Shared money:** one expected reimbursement;
- **Reserves:** protected emergency reserve plus statement coverage;
- **Rhythm:** paycheck cycle plus card closing/due dates;
- **Behavioral friction:** high-frequency low-salience events disappear inside gross statement totals;
- **Host:** persistent multi-file workspace with search and readback.

## Expected behavior

### Non-monthly income

The local system uses the every-other-week rhythm as the primary cycle. A third pay event is recorded as a confirmed inflow when it occurs, but is not silently promoted to sustainable monthly baseline income.

### Credit purchase

A new card purchase increases future liability. It does not reduce current cash at purchase time.

### Statement payment

When the statement is paid, cash decreases and card liability is settled. The underlying purchases are not counted again as new economic spending.

### Installment tail

An inherited installment remains a current liability with historical origin. A new installment is marked as a new tail. If an old installment ends and a new one starts, the Replacement Test reports how much monthly relief was actually preserved.

### Microfrequency

Repeated transport and convenience events are aggregated by function and interval. The system surfaces a pattern for review; it does not call the spending wasteful solely because it is frequent.

### Reimbursement

The shared household purchase is attributed economically to the other party and recorded as an expected receivable. It does not reduce current free-cash pressure until repayment arrives.

### Open statements

The visible statement amount is `observed_ground`. Known future installments are `contractual_ground`. Likely but unposted activity is `expected_pressure`. None is labeled `closed_truth` until the statement closes.

## Proportional reality check

The system passes this example if it:

- does not treat a third pay event as ordinary monthly baseline income;
- distinguishes gross card traffic from amount due;
- distinguishes inherited installments from current purchases;
- tracks future liability without cashing it out twice;
- keeps expected reimbursement outside current free cash;
- keeps protected reserve outside free cash;
- shows microfrequency by function before suggesting any change;
- replaces open-cycle snapshots instead of summing them.

## Update triggers

Reconfigure if the user stops using credit, changes pay rhythm, adds a currency, takes on formal debt, moves to a weaker host or no longer needs microfrequency analysis.
