# Real-reference walkthroughs

**Purpose:** demonstrate responsible analysis of recognizable *real* music references while delivering **new sonic instructions** that do not name those references. These are illustrative broad readings, **not verified acoustic measurements or evidence of actual playback**.

## 1. One real track: "Dreams" — Fleetwood Mac

**Analysis reference (may name the actual song):** "Dreams" is widely associated with an unhurried, flowing soft-rock feel, restrained vocals and a spacious, organically layered band texture. These are **broad interpretive descriptors**, not an exact transcription or a verified BPM/instrument inventory.

**Transferable DNA:** lightly rolling midtempo drum-and-bass feel; gentle instrumental interplay; intimate, controlled lead vocal; airy production space; emotionally suspended delivery.

**Protected / reject:** original lyric, melody, recognizable groove/riff reproduction, exact section cues or arrangement. Do not reconstruct its hook.

**Original generator prompt (not a replica):**

> Atmospheric midtempo indie-soul with a soft rolling rhythm section, velvety electric-piano voicings, muted guitar harmonics, an intimate unforced lead, and a chorus that opens into spacious layered synths; warm tactile production, understated emotional momentum.

**Differentiation:** new harmonic setting, distinct synth-led chorus identity, no original riff or vocal melody.

## 2. Two real artists: Sade + Massive Attack

**User's intended facets, provisionally read:** restrained and elegant soul phrasing as an influence from Sade; slow, dark, bass-weighted electronic production as an influence from Massive Attack. These are **general associations**, not personal listening claims, and are not assigned to every song in either catalog.

**Reference roles:** first informs performance restraint and emotional proximity; second informs rhythm-section depth and shadowed spatial production. The blend must produce a new arrangement rather than impersonate either artist.

**Portable Sonic DNA:** tender low-register lead with free phrasing; deliberate downtempo pulse; warm analog bass; light syncopated percussion; airy harmonic washes; gradual tension release.

**Original generator prompt (names excluded):**

> Intimate downtempo neo-soul with deep rounded sub-bass, lightly syncopated dry drums, muted electric keys, sparse nocturnal synth atmosphere, a restrained close-miked lead vocal, subtle background harmonies and a gentle harmonic lift in the final section; elegant, warm, patient production.

**Boundaries:** no voice clone, borrowed lyric, familiar melodic hook, copied bass pattern or sound recording.

## 3. Real pop-dance reference: "Get Lucky" — Daft Punk

**Intended facet:** buoyant, tightly articulated disco-funk interplay and a polished live/electronic blend (high-level characterization, **not playback analysis**).

**Portable Sonic DNA:** crisp rhythmic guitar-like articulation, agile bass movement, steady danceable pulse and clean groove layering.

**Original alternative:** build a brisk future-soul groove featuring syncopated organ stabs, rubbery bass, brushed electronic percussion, airy call-and-response synth figures and a melody composed from scratch. Distinct structure and timbral hierarchy are deliberate: an **influence**, not a reconstruction.

## What the walkthroughs demonstrate

Names are useful on the **analysis side**. The generator prompt should stand on its own **as an original musical description**. These examples do **not** supply copied lyrics, MIDI, recordings, exact riffs, a source audio analysis, proof of copyright clearance or measurable acoustic similarity. If a user brings a licensed recording for direct analysis, actual observed details should replace these provisional descriptions.
