# Synthetic Example: Informal Supporter Overload

This case is fictional and jurisdiction-neutral. It is a demonstration of architecture, not a real case study.

## The situation

Rowan, a fictional adult, currently depends on a sibling, Jordan, for transportation to appointments, form completion, medication reminders and communication with service offices. Jordan has begun missing work and cannot continue handling every function. Rowan wants to keep the relationship while making daily support safer and more sustainable.

## Reconstructed passage

```text
current state: four high-consequence functions are held by one sibling
desired state: Rowan retains decision-making and essential support continues
primary barrier: no formal owner for coordination and transport
secondary barriers: work schedule, limited time and uncertain service jurisdiction
minimum crossing: identify one intake door that can assess coordination and access needs
```

## Function and Dependency Map

| Function | Current holder | Risk if it stops | First route to investigate | Intended next owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transport | Jordan | Missed appointments | Local access or transport intake | Relevant service |
| Forms and calls | Jordan | Delays and lost requests | Support coordinator or service intake | Institution or coordinator |
| Medication reminders | Jordan | Missed doses | Clinical review of support need | Qualified healthcare owner |
| Appointment coordination | Jordan | Fragmented care | Service coordination intake | Named coordinator if available |

Jordan’s workload is part of the case state: work disruption, time pressure and inability to provide indefinite fallback. The map does not label Jordan as harmful or Rowan as incapable.

## Next step

The navigator would first resolve Rowan’s service area and identify an official intake route capable of assessing support coordination or accessibility. The first message should request assessment and routing, not promise a particular service.

Possible bounded script:

> Rowan is trying to maintain access to appointments and daily support. At present, transportation and coordination depend on one family member whose availability is no longer sustainable. Which service can assess these functional needs, and what is the correct intake route? If this is not the right channel, please provide a specific redirect and a way to record the request.

## Owner and checkpoint

- **Immediate owner:** Rowan, with Jordan’s consent and bounded assistance, identifies the relevant service area and confirms preferences.
- **Institutional owner to seek:** the service that can perform intake or redirect the request.
- **Checkpoint:** intake receipt, assessment date, specific redirect or confirmed failure of the first door.
- **Transfer test:** Jordan should no longer be the sole owner of every call, form and follow-up.

## What this example demonstrates

The useful change is not “remove Jordan.” It is to make the functions visible, name the supporter’s finite capacity, locate a door that can assume part of the work and make ownership observable.
