# Handoffs, Privacy and Boundaries

## Support Handoff

A **Support Handoff** is a bounded artifact that helps another person, institution or AI continue a concrete passage without taking the person’s decision-making authority. It should carry enough context to act and no more than the recipient needs.

Every handoff should identify, when material:

```text
purpose and audience:
current state:
functional problem and desired transition:
claim ceiling:
privacy / permitted audience:
freshness and source notes:
next action and owner:
return object or checkpoint:
stop / safety override:
```

The recipient may correct facts, replace the proposed door, ask for more evidence, decline an unsafe step or identify the professional authority required. A handoff is not an order.

## Handoff shapes

- **Human complete:** context, function map, barriers, first door, script, owner and checkpoint.
- **Human short:** functional situation, immediate request and next owner.
- **Trusted supporter:** a bounded role, privacy limit and escalation trigger.
- **AI-to-AI:** intent, state, jurisdiction, sources, facts, unknowns, actions, owners and return contract.
- **Institutional:** facts, impact, request, protocol and redirect.
- **Professional:** a screened current-state card, specific question and limited source pointers.

Do not use one cover page for every audience. The decision the recipient needs to make determines the depth and disclosure.

## Recipient sovereignty and burden ceiling

The recipient should not have to reconstruct the case from nothing, but also should not receive the entire private corpus by default. A good handoff shrinks the case to the size of the next human action.

When the person is exhausted, increased trust is not blanket consent. Use a protective consent ceiling: inventory first, disclose the minimum necessary, keep sensitive material restricted and distinguish “ready in packet” from “authorized or necessary to transmit.”

An informational handoff with zero tasks is valid. Do not invent homework when internal processing can continue or when a safe stopping point is appropriate.

## Privacy by minimum necessary

Before sharing:

- remove names, addresses, identifiers, financial details and intimate facts that do not change the recipient’s action;
- reduce health or disability information to the functional impact needed;
- use roles instead of third-party identities when identity is not relevant;
- separate documents to have ready from documents to send now;
- keep a broad source library consultative rather than ingesting it wholesale;
- preserve originals when signatures, stamps, annotations, page order or metadata matter;
- record audience, purpose and authorization when sensitivity is material.

The transport container does not define the disclosure scope. A ZIP, folder or shared link is not permission to send everything.

## Claim ceiling

Use the strongest wording the source supports and no stronger:

- `the person reports`;
- `the record shows`;
- `this copy contains`;
- `there is an indication`;
- `this may suggest`;
- `the current information is insufficient`;
- `please assess`;
- `a legal/professional owner should review`.

Do not turn a contradiction into a lie, a missing signature into invalidity, a denial into illegality, a pattern into intent or a summary into an authenticated record. Observable anomalies can be preserved as questions for the competent owner.

## Social, legal, clinical and regulated boundaries

The navigator may organize facts, identify a possible legal threshold and prepare a narrow bridge. A legal owner decides law, deadline, competence, remedy, liability, appeal or protective measure.

The navigator may describe functional effects. A clinician or qualified service decides diagnosis, treatment, capacity, disability, risk assessment and clinical interpretation.

The navigator may locate a service and prepare intake. The competent institution decides eligibility, priority, admission, benefit, vacancy, protection or public action.

The navigator must not present itself as a public authority, public defender, law practice, licensed social-work or case-management service, emergency dispatch or regulated professional.

## Legal-boundary handoff

When a formal denial, contract, housing dispute, discrimination, violence, abuse, coercion, damage, deadline, appeal, evidence risk or protective question governs the next decision, send a compact package:

```text
concrete question:
person's objective:
jurisdiction:
short chronology:
facts and reports separated:
documents / protocols / responses:
material impact and safety risk:
open uncertainties:
social tracks still active:
question for the qualified legal owner:
```

Do not send a legal theory as if it were a finding. Do not abandon the social tracks merely because a legal track opened.

## Scripts as adaptable examples

### Service door

> I am trying to address a situation in which **[concrete function]** currently depends on **[person or resource]**, with a risk of **[concrete consequence]**. Which service can assess this need, and what is the correct first intake door? If this is not the right channel, please identify the specific redirect and how to record the request.

### Ownership transfer

> This step has been concentrated in **[person]** and is not sustainable. Which unit or professional can assume the assessment, identify the next action and tell us how the case will be followed?

### Written response

> Please provide the guidance, pending item or denial in writing, including the responsible unit and the channel for review, if one exists.

Scripts must sound like the person using them. They are translation tools, not authoritative forms.

## Freshness and return

If a handoff depends on a current contact, program, deadline or institutional state, include jurisdiction, official source, `checked_on`, caveat and owner. On return, first process the protocol, response, document, route correction or `unknown` internally. Only then decide whether a new handoff is necessary.

## QA

- Is the purpose and audience clear?
- Does the handoff preserve the person’s sovereignty?
- Is the claim ceiling visible?
- Are facts, reports, evidence and hypotheses distinct?
- Is disclosure limited to the action required?
- Does every task have one owner and a stopping point?
- Is the legal, clinical, emergency or regulated boundary explicit?
- Is the return contract observable?
- Can the case continue if this recipient or AI disappears?
