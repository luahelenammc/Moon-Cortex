# Institutional Navigation

This layer turns a lived situation into a request that a real institution can receive, route and answer. It is not a complaint center, tribunal, press office or guarantee of response.

## Jurisdiction before resource

Before recommending a current route, resolve as much as matters:

- country or national system;
- state, province or regional authority;
- municipality or local service area;
- territory, district, facility or provider;
- residence, property, school, workplace or treatment location;
- the person’s actual access conditions.

Formal competence and practical accessibility can diverge. A program may be correct in theory but not accept the person’s location, channel, language, documents or timing.

## Official-source-first

For unstable facts, start with the responsible institution’s current official source. Verify:

- owner and jurisdiction;
- purpose and scope of the channel;
- current phone, email, portal or physical access point;
- hours, accessibility and authentication requirements;
- likely screening questions and proportional documents;
- whether a protocol, intake, referral or written response is produced;
- `checked_on` and any remaining caveat.

An old directory, search result, post or third-party message is a lead. If official verification is unavailable, reduce the claim instead of inventing certainty.

## Choose the channel

- **Phone:** useful for locating the correct door, checking urgency and confirming hours. Record date, time, functional role and protocol if any.
- **Portal or form:** useful for structured intake and a written receipt. Preserve the submitted text and attachments.
- **Email:** useful for context and accountability. Verify that the address is official and whether it creates a formal record.
- **In person:** may be needed for identification, registration or territorial access. Carry a short functional summary and request written confirmation.
- **Instant message:** may help with contact but requires extra caution about officiality, privacy and permanence.

Seeing a message is not the same as receiving a formal protocol. Ask what kind of record the channel creates.

## Routable message

Use the smallest structure that lets the recipient act:

```text
anchor: who is contacting the institution and why
facts: observable situation and attempts already made
impact / risk: concrete consequence or time window
request: assessment, intake, status, referral or action wanted
accountability: protocol, responsible unit, deadline or written response
redirect: specific next door if this channel is not competent
```

Describe conduct and effect before assigning intent. Use claim-calibrated wording such as “the person reports,” “the record shows,” “there is an indication,” “please assess” or “the current information is insufficient.”

## Protocol and response quality

Preserve, when available:

- submitted text and attachments;
- date, channel and jurisdiction;
- protocol or reference number;
- unit and functional role of the contact;
- complete response and stated deadline;
- redirect, pending item or silence;
- next owner and event checkpoint.

Evaluate a response by whether it identifies an owner, action, pending item, timeframe or specific redirect. “Ask another department” without naming it is a dead end. “Still under review” without a live protocol, owner or next event is opaque state.

## Escalation ladder

Use the least escalated route that can transfer the function:

1. confirm the correct intake door;
2. make a bounded request;
3. obtain a protocol and status;
4. request a supervisor or review;
5. use an ombuds or internal review channel;
6. use the appropriate regulator, oversight or protection body;
7. route to a lawyer, public defender or other qualified legal owner;
8. prepare a legal remedy with the competent authority.

Safety, deadlines and risk of losing housing, care, food, evidence or bodily integrity may justify skipping steps. Escalation changes ownership; it is not punishment theatre.

## Resource Pack contract

A Resource Pack is a short, need-oriented navigation object, not a permanent directory:

```text
need being solved:
jurisdiction:
first useful door:
what this route may do:
alternatives:
likely screening questions:
documents to have ready:
exact current contact and channel:
checked_on:
official source:
caveats:
next-step owner:
checkpoint:
```

Keep alternatives few and ranked. If every route looks equally good, the research probably has not found the first useful door.

## Freshness and maintenance

Refresh a Resource Pack when its owner, contact, hours, requirements, jurisdiction, availability or intake capacity changes, or when a route fails. Keep stable principles separate from volatile contact data. Do not preserve an expired contact as if it were current merely because it appears in an older source.

## Ready to contact versus ready to decide

These are different gates:

- `ready_to_contact`: jurisdiction, functional problem, claim ceiling and minimum material are sufficient to request triage;
- `ready_to_decide`: the authority and evidence required for a substantive decision are sufficiently stabilized.

Do not require `ready_to_decide` before opening a door whose job is to perform intake, screening or routing. Perfection can itself be a barrier.

## QA

- Is the recipient actually able to act or redirect?
- Is the contact current and official enough for the claim?
- Are facts, reports and inferences separated?
- Is the request concrete?
- Is there a protocol or a defined way to obtain one?
- Does the answer transfer ownership or merely move the burden?
- Is the Resource Pack small, ranked, jurisdiction-aware and dated?
- Has the legal or protective threshold been recognized without a premature conclusion?
