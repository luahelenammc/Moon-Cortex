# Support and Material Security

This layer asks what must be materially true for a person or household to continue safely. It does not prescribe a universal benefits catalog and it does not decide eligibility.

## Start from the function

Ask what is missing in practice:

- income or a predictable material floor;
- food and essential supplies;
- housing, utilities and physical safety;
- transport and accessibility;
- healthcare access, medication or continuity;
- care, rest and supporter relief;
- documentation and communication;
- education or work continuity;
- protection from violence, neglect, coercion or exploitation.

The same label can hide different routes. “Housing problem” may mean imminent loss of shelter, unsafe housing, inaccessible housing, a contract dispute, utility shutoff or a need to relocate without losing care. Reconstruct the concrete function and time horizon.

## Support layers

The module may consider a combination of:

1. **Material floor:** food, income, essential bills, medicine, transport and safe shelter.
2. **Care and access:** personal care, healthcare, rehabilitation, communication and accessibility support.
3. **Documentation:** identity, residence, income, household, medical or functional records only when needed for the route.
4. **Coordination:** a service, case coordinator or institutional owner capable of connecting tracks.
5. **Protection:** emergency, safeguarding, violence, abuse, neglect, coercion or exploitation response.
6. **Transition:** staged movement from informal dependence toward sustainable formal, community or self-directed support.

This is a map of possible functions, not a promise that every layer exists in every jurisdiction.

## Care, disability and autonomy

Describe effects on activities and participation when useful:

- daily activities such as hygiene, food, dressing, mobility, sleep and personal safety;
- instrumental activities such as medication, money, shopping, cooking, transport, communication, documents and scheduling;
- context such as pain, fatigue, crisis, language, technology, accessibility, predictability and environmental barriers.

This operational description does not constitute a clinical assessment. Diagnosis should be included only when necessary, authorized and relevant to the next decision. A family relationship does not automatically create authority over another person’s body, documents, income or choices.

## Supporter load

Map how often and how long an informal supporter performs each function and what it costs in time, money, work, health, sleep, housing, safety and emotional capacity. Love is not a renewable public utility. A plan that preserves one person’s survival by consuming another person’s basic stability is not secure.

For each function, ask:

```text
what happens now?
what fails if it stops?
what formal, community or technological support might absorb part of it?
what must remain with the person or chosen supporter?
who can become the next owner?
how will we know the transfer actually reduced load?
```

Replacing hands-on care with endless forms, phone calls and monitoring performed by the same supporter is administrative relocation of exhaustion, not function replacement.

## Material security map

When money, housing or care is at stake, record only what changes the route:

- current material situation and immediate risk;
- essential functions to protect first;
- known deadlines or windows;
- current informal dependencies;
- documents or records actually needed;
- likely institutional or community owners;
- fallback if the preferred route fails;
- owner and event checkpoint.

Budget and material facts are context for safety, not instruments of blame. Avoid making a person prove moral deservingness before naming the infrastructure they need.

## Protection and safety override

Violence, abuse, neglect, coercion, exploitation, retention of documents, control of medication, abandonment or immediate loss of essential shelter activates a protection route. Do not wait for a perfect support stack. Use the relevant emergency, protective, healthcare or institutional authority for the jurisdiction.

The navigator may identify a safety concern and prepare a bounded handoff. It must not conduct a validated risk assessment, make a clinical diagnosis, declare criminal responsibility or replace emergency services.

## Screening, application and decision

Keep these events separate:

- discovering a possible service;
- screening initial fit;
- applying or requesting intake;
- receiving a protocol;
- awaiting a decision;
- approval, denial or redirect;
- review, appeal or reapplication;
- legal or professional escalation.

“Worth investigating” is not “the person qualifies.” A denial is a fact to interpret with the competent authority; it is not automatically proof of illegality, nor proof that the person has no right.

## Relocation and continuity

Before recommending a move, check what would be lost or altered: benefits, care, treatment, transport, work, documents, safety, community and informal relationships. A cheaper or more available destination can still be a bad transition if the functions that made daily life possible do not travel with the person.

## QA

- Does the route answer a real function?
- Is the material floor protected enough for the next step?
- Was supporter load named without blaming the relationship?
- Are diagnosis and eligibility kept within their authority boundaries?
- Are documents proportional?
- Is there a formal, community or institutional owner where one is needed?
- Is the transition safer and more sustainable than the current concentration?
- Is a safety override available?
