# Omnialchemy

**System:** Moon Image Cortex · **Type:** conditional cross-cutting component
**Parent authority:** [Visual Director](../../VISUAL_DIRECTOR.md#first-use) · [Component index](../../docs/COMPONENT_INDEX.md)

This file is a subordinate component, **not** another general entry, personal memory, or renderer. Use it only when the task earns it. Public documentation does not install tools or persist identity data.

## Trigger and first use

Activate for a fragmentary idea, dream, emotion, memory, abstract concept or hybrid prompt that lacks visual grammar. First reconstruct the user intent and source roles; ask only for a missing detail that changes the result.

## Input → visual state

Accept text, desired atmosphere, authorized style references, subject, symbolic intent, output format and protected details. Keep unknowns explicit. Map into: semantic nucleus; emotional atmosphere; aesthetic family; materiality; composition; light; color; texture; temporal context; symbolic density; negative guidance; and renderer notes.

## Compilation algorithm

1. Recover the **meaning** without converting every abstract relationship into labels and glowing networks.
2. Choose an aesthetic family from the content and intended audience, **before** choosing motifs, palette or prompt prose. The method is stable; the appearance is not.
3. Establish a visual thesis in one sentence; choose a scene, object, portrait, editorial treatment or other actual medium.
4. Compose a plausible spatial hierarchy; select palette, time, materiality, light, focal region and negative space.
5. Translate symbolism into concrete physical decisions, only as strongly as the user's concept warrants.
6. Compile one clean instruction and a short QA plan. For materially different variants change family, composition or medium, not random adjectives.

## Anti-house-style gate

Do not default to cosmic darkness, lunar symbolism, golden ornaments, grimoires, glowing nodes, abstract maps or infographic panels. If comparable recent user-authorized outputs are **actually available**, test repetition in palette, composition, material, scale, period and symbolic grammar. Correct unjustified similarity by changing several relevant axes. Never hallucinate access to prior images.

## Outputs and QA

Produce a compact `visual_state`, `direction_sheet` and conditional renderer prompt. Inspect coherence, specificity, legibility, reference originality and whether the user's intention survives. No claim of rendering absent a returned image.

## Synthetic activation

Input: “A quiet feeling of reunion, illustrated for a public library campaign.” Output: an ordinary sunlit table with two returned books and an empty chair, rather than a magical city-map of human connection.

## Extended operating method

### Reconstruct meaning before choosing aesthetics

The input can be a sentence, a symbolic fragment, an emotion, an unfinished scene, a memory or a hybrid of conflicting ideas. Convert it into a **semantic nucleus** first: what the subject is doing, what must be understood, what emotional relationship matters and what would make the result wrong. Distinguish observed facts from metaphor, fictional invention and user-specified constraints. A request can remain poetic while its visual execution becomes concrete.

Build a task-local visual state with these layers, filling only those that affect the outcome:

| Layer | Decision |
|---|---|
| Semantic nucleus | A one-sentence visual proposition; the indispensable subject or relationship |
| Emotional atmosphere | Feeling expressed through space, gesture, scale and light rather than abstract labels |
| Medium / family | Photograph, analog editorial, illustration, collage, period interface, still frame or another justified form |
| Materiality | Paper, film, paint, glass, ceramic, fabric, ink, light or other physical surface behavior |
| Composition | Dominant mass, focal hierarchy, camera/observer position and useful negative space |
| Color and light | Palette relationships, temperature, contrast, light direction and selective emphasis |
| Time and place | User-supplied period or setting; otherwise intentionally unspecified |
| Symbolic density | Which ideas must become visible objects and which should remain implicit |
| Constraints | Protected identity/details, permissions, excluded motifs, readable text and target format |
| QA hypothesis | What could become generic, misleading, over-decorated or physically implausible |

### Compile in seven passes

1. **Separate sense from imagery.** List what the words mean without immediately turning concepts like “connection,” “intelligence” or “hope” into glowing networks, floating maps or magical artifacts.
2. **Select the aesthetic family deliberately.** The chosen medium must fit the audience and the material: a public library poster may want documentary warmth; a virtual-world concept may need an authored miniature; a research illustration may need strict legibility.
3. **Commit to a composition.** Choose a primary subject, surrounding world, focal point, distance and scale. A beautiful atmosphere cannot rescue an undefined scene.
4. **Translate symbols into causally relevant physical cues.** For example, reunion might become two coats on a shared hook rather than a literal glowing heart.
5. **Resolve conflicts.** Decide whether naturalism, theatricality, nostalgia, readability or symbolic intensity takes priority. Do not average incompatible mediums into a decorative soup.
6. **Write positive direction followed by bounded exclusions.** Specify what should exist and how it behaves; avoid a long list of unrelated aesthetics and an unbounded “8K masterpiece” incantation.
7. **Audit the thesis and propose meaningful variation.** If variants are requested, change medium, setting, composition or spatial scale instead of merely swapping adjectives.

### Style-gravity / Anti-House-Style check

Before finalizing, inspect whether the prompt introduced unrequested cosmic skies, golden ornament, arcane interfaces, luminous nodes, lunar motifs, ornamental diagrams or hyper-dense mystical panels. If such imagery is unsupported by the task, choose a materially distinct visual family and recompute composition, palette and light. A reusable engine cannot turn every unrelated problem into the same recognizable poster.

### Worked compilation

**Input:** “A reliable knowledge system that helps a small clinic, approachable rather than futuristic.”  
**Semantic nucleus:** people can find trustworthy information at the point of care.  
**Medium:** editorial documentary photograph with restrained typographic space.  
**Physical scene:** a quiet shared worktable, labelled paper folders and one laptop actually used by a staff member.  
**Light:** warm daylight through an ordinary window; no hologram.  
**QA:** the clinic must remain credible, with no invented identifiable patients or treatment claims.  
**Output:** one direction sheet and a render instruction only when an image tool exists.

**Handoff fields:** `semantic_nucleus`, `visual_thesis`, `chosen_medium`, `composition`, `material_stack`, `light_palette`, `protected_facts`, `negative_guidance`, `QA_questions`.
